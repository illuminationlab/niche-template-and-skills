# The Niche-Site Research Playbook

**What to research before building any niche site — and how to turn it into copy that ranks and converts.**

This is the general, niche-agnostic version of the research we've run across all five sites
(NeedleMoved · Rafter Elite · EngineGuild · Call and Crawl · WattsBooked). Follow it for any
new niche. It's built so you can hand it to anyone and they'll know exactly *what* to find,
*where* to look, and *what each finding is for*.

> **The one rule:** every claim on the finished site must trace back to something you found in
> research. If it feels like generic SaaS copy with the niche name swapped in, you skipped a step.

---

## How to use this document

Work top to bottom. Each **Module** below is a self-contained research job with:
- **Goal** — what you're trying to learn
- **Where to look** — the sources that actually have the answer
- **Questions to answer** — the specific things to write down
- **Feeds into** — which page(s)/decision the finding powers

Modules 1–6 are the raw research. Module 7 is how you synthesize it into positioning.
Module 8 is the SEO/keyword layer. Module 9 is the sanity check before you build.

---

## Module 1 — The niche's real pain points

**Goal:** Find the 3–5 problems that cost these owners real money and sleep — specifically
around getting customers, managing them, and following up.

**Where to look:**
- Reddit (the niche's subreddit + r/smallbusiness), industry forums, Facebook groups.
- 1★–3★ reviews of the tools they already use (that's where the unmet needs live).
- Trade blogs, YouTube comments on "running a [niche] business" videos.

**Questions to answer:**
- What's the #1 thing that makes them lose a job? (Usually: a missed call → the customer
  calls the next business on Google.)
- When do the highest-value jobs come in, and why do those get missed? (After-hours,
  weekends, while they're physically doing the work.)
- Where does money leak *after* the lead? (Quotes sent and never followed up, no-shows,
  no review requests, disorganized scheduling.)
- What are they doing manually that they hate? (Being receptionist + dispatcher + marketer
  + bookkeeper all at once.)

**Feeds into:** Homepage hero (lead with the #1 pain), Use Cases, the emotional through-line
of the whole site.

---

## Module 2 — The language they actually use

**Goal:** Capture the exact words practitioners use, so the copy sounds like an insider wrote it.

**Where to look:** Same forums/reviews as Module 1, but reading for *vocabulary*, not problems.

**Questions to answer:**
- What do they call themselves and their work? (e.g. electricians say "on the tools," "in a
  panel"; med spas say "clients," not "patients," in marketing.)
- What's the industry noun vs. the audience noun? (e.g. NICHE = "electrical contracting";
  AUDIENCE = "electricians." Write body copy to the audience noun.)
- What jargon signals you're legit, and what phrasing marks you as an outsider to avoid?

**Feeds into:** Every headline and body paragraph. Also the locked "audience phrasing rule"
in the research brief (how to refer to the reader).

---

## Module 3 — The competitor landscape and its gaps

**Goal:** Know who they already pay, what those tools do, and — critically — where those tools
fail this specific niche.

**Where to look:** The incumbent tools' own sites + their reviews (G2, Capterra, Reddit
"X vs Y" threads), plus the live Google SERP for "[niche] software" and "best [niche] CRM."

**Questions to answer:**
- Who are the 3–5 incumbents? (In field-service niches this is usually ServiceTitan,
  Housecall Pro, Jobber, FieldEdge; other niches have their own.)
- For each: who is it built for, roughly what does it cost, and what's the common complaint?
- Where's the gap we fit? The recurring one: incumbents are either **too expensive/complex for
  a small shop**, or they **bolt AI on as a pricey upper-tier add-on**. Our wedge is
  affordable + all-in-one + AI-native, built for one niche.
- Are there answering-service / receptionist competitors (human or AI) separate from the
  software players? (Often a whole second SERP worth owning — see WattsBooked's finding.)

**Feeds into:** Features "5 separate tools vs [PRODUCT]" comparison table, Pricing comparison,
About page ("underserved by generic tools"), and positioning (Module 7).

---

## Module 4 — Objections to switching (and to buying at all)

**Goal:** Surface the reasons they'll say no, so the copy can defuse each one.

**Where to look:** Reddit threads where someone asks "should I switch from X?", review
complaints about onboarding/migration, and "is it worth it" discussions.

**Questions to answer:**
- What makes switching software scary? (Data migration, learning curve, contracts, downtime.)
- What makes them skeptical of AI specifically? ("Will it sound like a robot to my
  customers?" "Will it book things wrong?")
- Price objections unique to this niche (seasonality, thin margins, solo operators).

**Feeds into:** Pricing FAQ (top 5 objections), Contact FAQ, reassurance copy, and the
AI-explainer sections (address the "robot" fear head-on).

---

## Module 5 — Who the buyers actually are (roles & scenarios)

**Goal:** Identify the 4–6 distinct business types/roles inside the niche so Use Cases speak
to real people, not "businesses."

**Where to look:** How the niche segments itself (solo operator vs. small crew vs. multi-tech
shop vs. commercial vs. residential), and which segment feels each pain hardest.

**Questions to answer:**
- What are the common business sizes/models? (Solo, small team, growing shop, commercial.)
- Residential vs. commercial vs. emergency — how do their needs differ?
- Which AI/automation scenario matters most to each? (e.g. solo → after-hours calls;
  growing shop → scheduling + dispatch; commercial → follow-up on big quotes.)

**Feeds into:** Use Cases page (one block per role, each with a scenario + outcomes + CTA).

## Module 6 — The startup path (for the "New [Niche] Business" page)

**Goal:** Understand how someone *starts* a business in this niche — the roadmap a brand-new
owner needs.

**Where to look:** "How to start a [niche] business" guides, licensing/certification body
sites, "startup cost" breakdowns, new-owner threads.

**Questions to answer:**
- Licensing, certification, and insurance actually required to operate legally.
- Realistic startup cost ranges (equipment, tools, vehicle, software).
- How new owners get their first customers (Google Business Profile, reviews, referrals).
- The systems a brand-new owner underestimates (answering calls, booking, follow-up).

**Feeds into:** The new "Starting a New [Niche] Business" page (Page 8) — see the separate
`PLAN-new-business-page.md`.

## Module 7 — Synthesis: the positioning statement

**Goal:** Compress Modules 1–5 into one paragraph that drives the whole site. This is the
single most important output — everything else is evidence for it.

**Write one paragraph that names:**
1. The core, money-losing pain (from Module 1).
2. When/why it hurts most (the after-hours / on-the-job moment).
3. The downstream leaks (unfollowed quotes, no reviews, owner burnout).
4. Why the incumbents don't solve it for *this* buyer (too expensive/complex, or AI is a
   costly add-on).
5. The one-line answer: **[PRODUCT] is the affordable, all-in-one, AI-powered system built
   only for [niche].**

> **Template** (adapt, don't copy): *"[Audience] lose real money every time [core pain
> moment]. The caller doesn't leave a voicemail — they call the next [niche] business on
> Google. [Downstream leaks]. The incumbents ([names]) are either too expensive and complex
> for a small shop, or bolt AI on as a costly upper tier. [PRODUCT] is the affordable,
> all-in-one, AI-powered system built only for [audience]."*

**Feeds into:** Homepage subheadline, About page, and the tone of every other page.

## Module 8 — The SEO / keyword layer (run LIVE, never from memory)

**Goal:** Build the keyword map that assigns a primary + secondary keyword set to each page,
based on *real* volume and difficulty — not guesses.

**How to run it:**
- Use **live keyword data** (OpenSEO / DataForSEO) every time. Volumes and difficulty shift;
  don't reuse an old list. (See the `seo-optimizer` skill.)
- Seed from the niche's core intents: `[niche] software`, `[niche] CRM`,
  `[niche] scheduling software`, `[niche] answering service`, `ai receptionist for [niche]`,
  `how to start a [niche] business`.
- For each candidate, capture **search volume, keyword difficulty (KD), search intent, and
  CPC**. High CPC = high buyer value even at low volume — those are money terms.

**How to choose (the winnability filter):**
- Prefer **KD ≤ 20 + commercial/transactional intent + relevance**.
- Check the **live SERP** before committing: a low KD score means nothing if page one is all
  funded incumbents. Look for SERPs full of *beatable* pages (generic directories, old blog
  posts, non-niche-native players). The "[niche] answering service" cluster is often KD 0 and
  wide open even when "[niche] software" is crowded.
- Don't chase competitor-brand navigational terms (their logins, their names) — no value.

**How to assign:**
- One primary keyword per page + 3–5 secondary. Map the highest-value winnable cluster to a
  dedicated page. Long-tail informational terms → blog posts in Resources.

**Feeds into:** The SEO block at the top of every page (meta title ≤60, meta description ≤160),
the H1/H2 structure, and which blog posts to write.

---

## Module 9 — Pre-build sanity check

Before any page gets built from the research, verify:
- [ ] Positioning paragraph (Module 7) is written and sharp.
- [ ] Every page has a primary keyword + secondaries backed by **real** numbers (Module 8).
- [ ] Copy uses the audience's real language (Module 2), not generic SaaS filler.
- [ ] No fabricated statistics, no invented company names, no made-up percentages.
- [ ] No free-trial language anywhere (house rule).
- [ ] Testimonial stand-ins are first name + last initial + city only — qualitative, no fake
      numbers, no real business names.
- [ ] Costs/prices cited as honest ranges, not fabricated exact figures.
- [ ] AI "robot" fear is addressed directly somewhere (Module 4).

## The house constraints (apply to every niche, every time)
- Product is GHL, white-labeled and positioned exclusively for one niche.
- Full GHL feature set is fair game; the two AI tools (Voice AI receptionist +
  Conversation AI chat) get explained in detail and referenced throughout.
- Pricing framed as affordable base + usage-based ("only pay when it's working for you").
- No free trials. No fabricated stats. Insider language, not generic SaaS.
- House brand: teal `#00b0b8` + navy `#002445` unless the niche explicitly overrides.
